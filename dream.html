Chapter 1: Chad’s Dream

Dumia and the Dream

Dumia was a peaceful village where mornings began with roosters and evenings ended with children playing football on dusty fields. Chad, the only son of Andrew and Isabella, was always the first child on the field and the last to leave. His football was old, but his dream was enormous. He wanted to play for Real Madrid and become a famous footballer.

Andrew often watched from a distance. He knew his son was talented, but he also knew the family could not afford a professional academy. Isabella encouraged Chad anyway. She reminded him that a poor beginning did not have to mean a poor ending.

Chad practiced every day. He learned to control the ball with both feet, pass accurately, and move around defenders. His friends sometimes laughed at the way he talked about playing in Europe. Chad never became discouraged. He believed that dreams were supposed to be bigger than the place where a person started.

One afternoon, a wealthy football scout named Mr. Daniel happened to pass Dumia. He stopped when he saw Chad playing. The scout watched several minutes without speaking. Chad dribbled past two older boys, changed direction, and scored.

The scout approached Andrew afterward and asked whether Chad had ever attended an academy. Andrew explained that the family could not afford one.

The scout offered to sponsor Chad's enrollment at FC Monaco.

The news changed everything.

Chad thought about the difference between the life people saw and the life he actually lived.
The public saw the footballer, the trophies, the cars and the success. They did not see the nights he had cried because he missed home, the pressure before important matches, or the memories of the people who had helped him begin.
That difference became one of the most important lessons in his life.

In dumia and the dream, Chad did not simply move from one event to another.
He had to make choices, think about their consequences, and deal with the feelings those choices created.
He sometimes questioned whether he was doing the right thing. When that happened, he returned to the principles his parents had taught him: work hard, respect people, and do not allow success to make you forget where you came from.

Those principles helped him understand what was happening around him. Instead of judging people only by their appearance, he began looking at their actions.
Instead of assuming that every problem could be solved with money, he learned that some problems required patience and honesty.

That lesson followed him into the next stage of his life.

Whenever Chad faced a difficult choice, he remembered Dumia. The village had taught him that a person's circumstances could change without changing the person's worth.
He had once been the boy with little money but enormous ambition. Now he had money, but he wanted to remain the same person inside.

Leaving Home

Chad was excited but frightened when the time came to leave Dumia. Andrew told him that opportunity required courage. Isabella packed his clothes and spent the night giving him advice.

At the bus station, Chad promised his parents that he would return successful.

Training at FC Monaco was harder than Chad expected. Talented players surrounded him. Some had trained since childhood, while Chad was still learning how to compete at that level. His first weeks were filled with mistakes.

One coach told him, “Talent opens the door. Discipline keeps you inside.”

Chad took those words seriously.

He woke early, trained after scheduled sessions, studied matches, and listened to experienced players. Gradually, he improved.

He made friends and learned how to deal with homesickness. Whenever he missed home, he called Isabella.

She always ended their conversations with the same message: “Keep going.”

Two years passed. Chad became one of the academy's standout players. Scouts began attending his matches. Then one evening, the director called him into an office.

There was a message from Real Madrid.

Chad could hardly believe it.

The club wanted him.

The Road to Madrid

Moving to Real Madrid was another huge adjustment. The stadiums were larger, the training sessions were intense, and the expectations were enormous.

Chad sometimes sat alone after training and wondered whether he belonged among players he had watched on television as a child.

His first opportunity came unexpectedly when another player was injured. Chad entered the match late. He played with confidence, assisted a goal, and helped his team win.

Afterward, his teammates congratulated him.

His name began appearing in sports reports.

But fame also brought pressure. People judged his performances. A poor match could become the main topic of discussion the following morning.

Chad learned to remain focused.

He remembered Dumia.

He remembered his father repairing old football boots.

He remembered Isabella telling him never to be ashamed of his beginning.

Four years passed quickly.

Chad was now a professional star.

Then the World Cup arrived.

The Call

Before one of the biggest matches of his career, Chad called his mother.

At first, Isabella sounded normal.

Then she became quiet.

“Chad, there is something you need to know.”

She told him that Andrew had died.

The news shattered him.

Chad sat alone and cried. His father had been there before the fame, before the money, before Real Madrid.

He wanted to go home immediately.

Isabella told him that Andrew would not want his son to abandon his dream.

“Stand up,” she said. “Play for him.”

Chad returned to training.

He played the World Cup match with his father's memory in his heart. His team won the tournament.

When Chad lifted the trophy, he did not think about the cameras.

Chapter 2: The Price of Fame

A Different Life

It had grown.
